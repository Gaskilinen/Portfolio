Exotic Cross-Sell

Research showed customers didn't know what an exacta was, despite placing those bets with fixed odds. By educating and signposting customers, I increased the number of bets on better customer value and higher profit margin bet types by 8%.

01 Context

The Problem

"I do my forecasts with skybet, why should I do an exacta with the Tote?"

The Tote have different naming conventions compared to fixed odds – exacta / forecast, trifecta / tricast. This creates a barrier to entry for customers as they will not recognise they're the same bet. Moreover, the Tote typically pays out more than fixed odds for those bets so they are better value for customers.

From a business perspective, exactas and trifectas are one of the highest margin pools so it is important to try and grow them. Furthermore, win is one of the smallest margin pools, so when there is a big favourite, signposting an exacta over a win will be more financially beneficial for the business.

02 North Star

Goals & Success Metrics

Design Goal:
(1) Create a cross-sell section that can be used for any bet type, (2) educate customers on what the other bet types are, (3) highlight the value of exactas and trifectas.
Business Goal:
Increase the bets placed in higher margin pools.
Success Metrics:
Number of bets in exacta / trifecta.
03 Discovery

Research & Discovery

I collated evidence using interviews from other initiatives where customers have referenced exactas, in total I used clips from four different participants to gain an insight into the problem.

Insights

Education

"I don't know what an exacta or trifecta is."

When the industry name for these bets are forecast and tricasts, customers will be expecting the same naming conventions with the Tote. The issue is the Tote's naming conventions are a legacy whereas fixed odds naming is fairly recent.

Awareness

"I didn't know you could do a forecast with the Tote."

The bets are hidden behind a tab. Whilst the naming is different the racecard UI is familiar, but customers aren't clicking through to see.

Value

"I do my forecasts with Skybet, why should I do them with the Tote?"

The Tote's exacta pays out higher than a fixed odds forecast more often than not, however customers don't know this.

Competitor analysis showed that above the racecard is a prime location for cross-selling customers into other bet types.

Often the returns are highlighted as a value proposition, therefore it is a good opportunity for the Tote to showcase the value of the exacta and trifecta.

04 Design

Exploration

Horizontally scrolled cards had the disadvantage that the amount of content will be different dependent on the bet, so there would be a lot of empty space.

A singular component for all the bets impacted the touch affordance and took up too much room. Describing the bet would help the education but customers would not read the entire sentence and would scan the content.

Replacing the names of the betslip with an explanation would help educate customers, as well as listing the horses' positions so a customer can scan the content.

Adding the will-pay would also show the customer the value of the bets.

05 Solution

User Journey

Key design decisions

  1. Choosing which horses to show in the component – I wanted to make the component manageable by our operations team so they can choose which combinations based on price to display in that component, however because of resource constraints that was not possible so we automated it based on the top of the market.
  2. Resource – Surfacing the will-pays required backend resource that was not available. I proposed that showing the value of the bets was the key paint-point solution. Therefore the initiative was broken into two deliverables after discussions with engineering.
06 Validation

Validation

Usability Testing

Existing Exacta Journey

Proposed Exacta Journey

Usability testing results showed that having a component above the racecard reduced the difficulty for the customer to place exactas & trifectas, thus improving the awareness that was identified as a customer pain point.

  • "I found the prototype A easier to navigate and place the bet."
  • "I like to have all options presented to me and not just things like boosts like prototype A appeared to be doing."
  • "It was simpler to place the bet as it was just one button on the very first page, which explicitly told me exactly what to do."

Business Case

I worked closely with the PM to build a business case for the customer problem to get it prioritised and into the delivery roadmap.

The trigger for this initiative was the business looking for tactical items to add to the roadmap as they wanted quick revenue drivers to hit targets. My discovery research and usability testing provided a compelling argument as I presented my findings and designs to SLT to get business buy-in.

09 Looking ahead

Next Steps

Following the success of creating prepopulated exacta and trifecta bets, it is a proof of concept that customers react well to the convenience of not having to build their own bets.

Therefore, I can begin to explore signposting boosted win bets, as well as building social proofing and prepopulated placepots, with the expectation of being able to increase the number of bets placed.
