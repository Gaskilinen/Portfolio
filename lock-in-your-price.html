Lock in your price

Revolutionising pools betting by allowing customers to 'take a price' after research showed that not having guaranteed returns was the biggest obstacle to customer adoption.

ROLE
Lead Designer
RESPONSIBILITIES
  • Identified the problem through surveys.
  • Conducted internal usability testing with colleagues.
  • Conducted 10 user interviews with existing customers and 'prospect customers'.
  • Redesigned the Racecard and Betslip.
  • Collaborated closely with CSO, senior stakeholders, and product for design and research strategy.
COLLABORATORS
UX Design, Product, Engineering, SLT, C-Suite, BI, Product Analyst, Business Planning, Head of Racing.
TIMELINE
Key Strategic item for FY 2025–26 (Sept–Sept).
01 Context

The Problem

"When I place the bet the dividend is paying 5.00, but when the race goes off you suddenly change it to 3.50"

Customers have voiced their frustration that they initially do not know what returns they will get for their bet and that the dividend when they placed the bet can be vastly different from what they will receive.

New customers will have some level of experience with fixed odds betting, therefore they will be expecting to be receiving the dividend payout from when they placed the bet. By breaking these expectations new customers become confused and do not stick.

02 North Star

Goals & Success Metrics

Design Goal:
Design a racecard that does not compromise the experience for existing customers that enjoy betting in the pool yet building a parallel experience for customers that have a need of taking a fixed price.
Business Goal:
Increase customer ASPU.
Success Metrics:
Retention Rates, Bets per Active, Stake per Active, Win Bet Conversion.
03 Discovery

Research & Discovery

  1. Quarterly surveys identified that not offering fixed odds was an obstacle to customers using the Tote.
  2. A focused survey on taking a price was sent to the Tote Bet Club, a research group with some of our most engaged customers. 49% would take a worse price if their returns were guaranteed over the pool price.
  3. Collaborated with senior stakeholders to create a research plan where we would use interviews to get feedback from existing and prospect customers. The latter would require further input from marketing on how we recruit through our partners.
  4. Tested the validity of the interview questions and reliability of the prototype through internal usability testing. Presented the findings to stakeholders and agreed timelines for interviews.
  5. Conducted customer interviews with 6 existing customers, played the results back to the Lock in Your Price working group.
  6. Next Step… Create an interactive prototype with Claude to test with a wider sample group. Also, based on initial findings, I have created a prospect customer survey as the business want more clarity on what Lock in Your Price is potentially worth.
04 Design

Exploration

Feedback for differentiating the pricing by decimal and odds was that it was difficult to compare the value of the two prices.

Secondly, we looked at having the Pool and Price Lock within separate tabs and just exposing the top of the market. Feedback from the CSO was that Price Lock must be pushed for the business case to work.

Icons were too subjective and not immediately obvious what they meant.

Our current iterations uses labels over iconography as well as colours to differentiate to the customer which is pool and which is PL. This iteration was accepted by stakeholders to be researched with customers.

05 Solution

User Journey

Key design decisions

  1. Racecard Redesign – Applied minimalist design principles to make the racecard look less 'cluttered', which had the further benefit of bringing more runners above the fold.
  2. Sticky Headers – Making it so the race times stick at the top of the page will make it easier for customers to add more selections to their betslip from different races – which has a higher margin for the business.
  3. Animation – I added animation on the bet receipt to empathise the success messaging from the system that the customer has completed an action.
06 Validation

Validation

6 interviews with Tote customers were conducted, that resulted from collaboration from key stakeholders for the research plan. There were 5 key objectives for the interviews:

  1. Gather insight into whether customers find it clear which option is the pool and which is LIYP.
  2. Gather feedback on racecard redesign.
  3. Gather insight into whether customers can see what their returns will be for a LIYP bet.
  4. Gather feedback on the multiples experiences, within one meeting and across meetings.

Insights

Lock in Your Price is a good thing, but 3/6 selected the locked value

The functionality was attractive to customers, but with having two prices next to each other, they were more likely to select the bigger value which was the pool.

Biggest motivator to using lock in your price is value

This contradicts the view of stakeholders that customers aren't price sensitive. However, the sense of value is subjective, so how do we make customers feel that they're getting value for their money.

Further research required

The static racecard meant it didn't represent what customers would be using in real life, with the prices constantly changing which would impact on their decision making.

Iterations

  1. Reduced the amount of 'brand' red on the racecard, because of the clash with errors, but mainly so the main CTA stands out on the page.
  2. Customers didn't realise you could click the price to change it to the pool on the betslip, so a toggle will be used to make it more visible.
  3. View interactive prototype →

The next round of research with existing customers would be using an interactive prototype, that would replicate real life pricing. This is because a static figma prototype does not include showing a price shortening or drifting, two factors that influence whether a customer wants to 'lock in' a price.
