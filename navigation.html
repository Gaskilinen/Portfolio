Navigation

Introduced a left panel navigation to improve navigation, as the previous drop down was landing customers on races that they did not intend to bet on. The panel enabled signposting 'featured' pages, which has 1.5M events in 6 months.

ROLE
Lead Designer
RESPONSIBILITIES
  • Identified the problem through surveys and using clips from other initiative's interviews.
  • Collaborated with BI to build dashboards displaying conversion and number of clicks to achieve goals.
  • Conducted competitor analysis, also used NN to understand best practices.
  • Conducted A/B tests to validate design.
COLLABORATORS
UX Design, Product, Engineering, BI, Product Analyst, Operations.
TIMELINE

01 Context

The Problem

Customers were not in control of where they were navigating to when they clicked on a 'single leg' bet.

  1. Customer would click on the bet type drop down.
  2. Customer then clicks on win/place.
  3. Customer lands on the next chronological race.
  4. Customer then clicks meeting drop down.
  5. Customer then clicks on the race they wanted to navigate to.
02 North Star

Goals & Success Metrics

Design Goal:
Reduce the amount of clicks for the customer to achieve their goals. Increase the traffic of customers going to key areas of the website.
Business Goal:
Increase the amount of bets placed per customer.
Success Metrics:
Win conversion rates, number of impressions for key pages.
03 Discovery

Research & Discovery

  1. Quarterly surveys identified that navigation was a key problem for customers.
  2. Competitor research showed that left hand panels were common place within the industry and used to highlight popular / featured events.
04 Design

User Journey

Before

All single leg bets in the dropdown would navigate to the next chronological race, including international races. Our customers primarily bet on domestic races and would voice their frustration at being taken to racing that they have no interest in.

Redesign

Key design decisions

  1. Removing the win/place bet type – competitor analysis showed that to navigate to place a win bet, punters must go through the racecards. Therefore, customers will expect the same behaviour when using the Tote, meaning they can be in control of what race they navigate to.
  2. Featured – Collaborated with the Ops team about what to populate in the featured area. We agreed on football and in-play being a constant whilst creating the requirement that they can add any sport or event they feel appropriate.
  3. Mobile View – Building a burger menu with the left hand panel's content. As there is less signposting if a customer wanted to navigate via a bet type, we agreed to an A/B test to measure if the tabs were still effective for mobile.
05 Validation

AB Testing

Using LaunchDarkly and working with BI, we created an A/B test by using a cohort of active win bettors against a control of the existing journey.

We used the Tote Bet Club research cohort to test the new layout, they showed an increase in win bet conversion with no impact to the number of bets.

06 Impact

Results & Impact

The left panel navigation has enabled us to create content managed components, where our operations team can select popular sports and events to be signposted for customers. This has had an added benefit of increasing the amount of traffic for customers. Furthermore, for win bets customers are navigating by selecting their desired time on the meeting component, meaning the win racecard is now one click away.

07 Reflections

Reflections and Learnings

Technology Limitations

With the sportsbook being a 3rd party integration, the options for combining the Tote homepage and the sports page are limited.

What I did:

  • Persuaded stakeholders that making it easier for customers to navigate to our sportsbook, as by signposting the entry points to the sportsbook would boost conversion at a low cost would be a compelling business case.

What I learned:

  • Working closely with the developers to understand the limitations and opportunities with not just the Tote, but also third party priorities.
08 Looking ahead

Next Steps

"I didn't know you had a sportsbook" – app user

The changes were isolated to the website, as the app did not have the resource to improve the navigation. Therefore, the next step is to replicate this navigation on the apps.

The Tote platform and the third party sportsbook can only be integrated through links, rather than widgets or a native component.

To work around this issue, I created another level of navigation for the app that includes the most popular sports for our customers, as currently the only signposting for other sports is a button on the footer.

With the web proving the concept that customers will click on links to football, I want to replicate that behaviour on the apps.
